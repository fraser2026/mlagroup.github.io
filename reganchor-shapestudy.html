Four ways to anchor.

The first pass was a mountain — well-made, but that's Vanta's silhouette, not RegAnchor's. These four come from what "anchor" actually means outside a ship: the piece that locks a structure, the sample that proves what's inside, the fixed point everything else is measured against. Same facet-and-grain system throughout. Same six tones. Different problems, different shapes.

02The vocabulary

None of these are a ship's anchor. All of them are what "anchor" means.

Rule 4 in RGA-001 rules out the literal icon. That still leaves the entire engineering and surveying vocabulary of the word — arguably a better fit for a governance product than a nautical symbol would ever have been.

Keystone
Architecture. The one stone in an arch that carries load from both sides — pull it and everything else fails. Best where you need to say "this is the piece that makes the rest hold."
Core
Geology / engineering. A cylindrical sample drilled out to show exactly what's inside, layer by layer — the diagonal top is the cut face. This is what an assessment does to a governance programme. Best for tall, narrow placements.
Datum
Surveying. The fixed reference point every other measurement is taken from — the actual technical term for "anchor point" in the sense you mean it. Best for seals, marks, anywhere small and singular.
Strata
Geology. Layered rock, base to summit — kept from the first pass because the seven-course count still ties directly to the Compliance Bar's seven levels. Best as the large, quiet hero moment.
