The Problem with Generic Assessments

Generic maturity models
don't hold up
under examination.

Most AI governance tools produce a score. Regulators do not accept a score. They examine documentation, accountability structures, decision logs, and the specific legal basis for each governance control.

The MLA framework was built backwards from that examination — starting with what the FCA, ICO, and EU AI Act actually require, then designing the instrument to surface exactly those gaps.

Every question maps to a specific article, guidance paragraph, or regulatory expectation. Every finding in the output carries the legal citation that makes it usable in a board pack or supervisory submission.

Scoring Engine

How a score becomes
a defensible position.

01
Pre-Screen Context

Before any question is answered, the instrument collects sector, organisation size, and active regulatory regimes. These are not cosmetic — they directly modify how scores are calculated and which flags are automatically triggered.

Sector Exposure Multiplier
02
Weighted Question Scoring

Each of the 35 questions scores 0–3. Answers of "Unsure" or "Don't know" score zero and trigger an uncertainty flag — which can elevate the risk band independently of the numeric score. Partial compliance is explicitly distinguished from no compliance.

0–3 Per Question
03
High-Risk Category Adjustment

Organisations operating high-risk AI categories — as defined by EU AI Act Annex III — face harder section maximums in the domains most affected. Identical answers produce a lower percentage for a firm using biometric AI than one using only internal operational tools.

EU AI Act Annex III
04
Sector Exposure Multiplier

The weighted score is multiplied by a sector-specific factor. Financial services and healthcare carry the highest multipliers — reflecting the density of AI-specific obligations those regulators impose. This produces the Adjusted Exposure Score that determines the risk band.

Sector-Calibrated Output
05
Priority Flags

Certain answers trigger Priority Flags that appear in the report regardless of the overall score. A high total score does not suppress a Priority Flag. These represent binary legal obligations — no human oversight of AI decisions is a UK GDPR Article 22 issue, not a governance maturity question.

Cannot Be Mitigated by Score
06
Five-Band Risk Assignment

The Adjusted Exposure Score maps to one of five bands. If three or more uncertainty flags have been triggered, the assigned band is elevated regardless of the numeric score. The output includes the risk band narrative, domain breakdown, priority flags, and a sequenced remediation roadmap.

Board-Ready Output
Risk Band Logic

Five bands.
One deliberate
design decision.

Four-band risk instruments share a common failure: the middle band gets read as acceptable. "Moderate risk" sounds manageable. Organisations stay there.

The fifth band eliminates that comfort zone. There is no middle — only "foundations present" or "gaps exist." Every band heading is written to communicate regulatory consequence, not a maturity level. The language is precise and intentional.

Scoring thresholds are proprietary

The exact numeric boundaries between bands, sector multipliers, and uncertainty elevation thresholds are not published. This is intentional — organisations should not be able to reverse-engineer a passing score.

Regulatory Alignment

Every finding is
legally grounded.

EU AI Act
Annex III High-Risk Detection

Automatic identification of high-risk categories under Annex III. Conformity assessment flags and Article 14 human oversight obligations mapped directly from responses. High-risk obligations in force from August 2026.

UK GDPR
Art. 22 Automated Decisions

Article 22 prohibits solely automated decisions with significant effects without appropriate safeguards. Article 35 mandates DPIAs for high-risk AI processing. Individual rights under Chapter III are tested across multiple questions.

FCA SM&CR / DP5/22
Named Accountability

FCA DP5/22 and PS7/21 expect AI risk embedded in operational risk frameworks with named Senior Manager accountability. Consumer Duty requires boards to receive MI on AI-driven outcomes. Absence of named governance ownership is a Priority Flag.

ICO Guidance
Accountability Framework

ICO's Accountability Framework requires documented policies, privacy by design, and records of processing. ICO AI and Data Protection Guidance 2023 sets expectations for automated decision-making transparency and explainability.

ISO/IEC 42001
AI Management Systems

The international standard for AI management systems. Provides the structural backbone for governance documentation, risk assessment cycles, and continuous improvement requirements that complement the regulatory obligations above.

SRA / CQC / NHS
Sector-Specific Obligations

SRA AI Guidance 2024 requires disclosure of AI use to clients. NHS DTAC and DCB0129 apply to clinical AI. CQC oversight triggers specific flags in healthcare deployments. Sector selection in the pre-screen activates the relevant mandatory obligations.
